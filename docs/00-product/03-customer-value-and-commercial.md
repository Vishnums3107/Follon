# Customer, value, and commercial model

## Initial customer

- Independent systematic traders.
- Technically sophisticated discretionary traders.
- Small proprietary teams and funds with one to five traders.
- Developers replacing a collection of notebooks, spreadsheets, broker terminals, charts, and scripts.

Beginning retail traders are deliberately not the first audience: they need education and support rather than the reproducibility and controls this product is designed to sell.

## Commercial wedge

Sell reliability, control, and research-to-production velocity—not indicators, AI predictions, or promised returns. The early proof is a design partner who can complete normal paper-trading workflows without direct developer help.

## Founder-led validation sequence

Commercial evidence is a prerequisite for product expansion, not a task left
until the commercial-controls phase:

1. Observe at least ten target users completing their current research-to-paper
   workflow. Record the tools, elapsed time, failures, workarounds, buyer, and
   cost of the problem; a feature request without an observed workflow is not
   evidence.
2. Identify one repeated job and recruit at least three design partners who
   agree to use the same bounded Release 1 workflow. Record why each person is
   in the target segment and what would cause them to stop using it.
3. Demonstrate the deterministic replay and paper evidence package using the
   partner's own non-sensitive workflow. Track activation, unaided task
   completion, time-to-first-result, support interventions, unexplained
   discrepancies, and weekly continued use.
4. Ask for a paid, time-bounded pilot before adding another asset class,
   broker, or enterprise control. A letter of intent or verbal enthusiasm does
   not count as payment.
5. Complete the existing five-partner unaided-use gate, then the ten-paying-
   professional or three-paying-organization gate. Source code, fixtures, and
   tests cannot satisfy either gate.

The initial trust package should lead with reproducible results, explicit
non-live/live boundaries, broker reconciliation, a readable audit export,
credential containment, incident/restore procedures, and a clear list of
unsupported behavior. Security certifications, a broad options surface, and
self-hosting remain hypotheses until a buyer says they are purchase blockers.

After each 12-week discovery/pilot cycle, continue only if evidence shows a
repeated target-user problem, at least three qualified design partners, and at
least one credible paid-pilot path. Otherwise narrow the workflow, change the
segment, or stop commercial expansion; do not answer weak demand with more
infrastructure.

## Pricing hypotheses, not launch commitments

| Tier | Indicative position |
| --- | --- |
| Professional | $199–$399/month for one user and one live account |
| Quant | $499–$999/month for advanced data workflows, multiple strategies, and options analytics |
| Team | $12k–$30k/year for multiple users, permissions, approvals, central risk, and audit export |
| Private deployment | $50k+/year for customer-controlled infrastructure, enterprise auth, retention, and support |

Validate willingness to pay with design partners before setting public prices. Treat a seven-figure valuation as a business-quality outcome—retention, recurring revenue, IP ownership, security, operational documentation—not a feature requirement.

Record quoted price, approved price, buyer, procurement obstacle, start date,
renewal decision, and churn reason. Only contracted recurring revenue belongs
in ARR or valuation claims.
