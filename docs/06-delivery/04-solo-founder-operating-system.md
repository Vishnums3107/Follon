# Solo-founder operating system

Solo operation is a constraint on active scope, not proof that the complete
reference architecture is achievable by one person in 24 months. Keep only one
external gate and one supporting engineering outcome active at a time. Work
requiring simultaneous production engineering, independent security review,
legal/compliance ownership, and enterprise sales must be narrowed, sequenced,
or staffed explicitly.

## Sustainable allocation

| Focus | Allocation |
| --- | --- |
| Core product engineering | 50% |
| Automated testing and reliability | 20% |
| Security and operations | 10% |
| Documentation | 10% |
| Customer research and support | 10% |

Testing is not spare capacity to trade for more visible features.

## Weekly cadence

- **Monday:** review incidents/risk, select one outcome, update the design document.
- **Tuesday–Thursday:** implement, test, benchmark, and document together.
- **Friday:** run replay and fault-injection suites, security scans, a release candidate/demo, retrospective, and decision log.

Limit work in progress to one major feature.

## Runway and stop rules

Maintain a monthly runway record outside the repository. Calculate usable
runway as liquid funds allocated to the project divided by total monthly burn,
including personal essentials, business costs, tax/insurance, data and broker
fees, legal/security review, and a contingency allowance. Do not count
uncontracted revenue, an expected valuation, or emergency savings reserved for
another purpose.

Before starting each gate, write down its maximum cash cost, maximum elapsed
time, required external reviewers, success evidence, and stop date. Preserve a
separate six-month personal/employment-transition reserve. If the remaining
project runway cannot cover the next experiment plus that reserve, pause new
production scope and choose among paid consulting/employment, a smaller
research-only product, outside capital, or stopping the project.

Review runway, health, support load, and concentration risk every month. A
missed gate is a re-scope decision, not a reason to spend the reserve or reduce
testing, security, or reconciliation quality.

## Design partners

Starting in month four, interview two professional traders every month. Observe current workflows, record repetitive manual work and actual errors, then prioritize only demonstrated pain. Charge early enough to distinguish interest from willingness to pay.

Use the stronger evidence and stop criteria in the
[commercial validation sequence](../00-product/03-customer-value-and-commercial.md).
Until that sequence produces qualified design partners, customer discovery and
the bounded replay-to-paper workflow take priority over later roadmap features.
